Method

How the model works

Score every season

Every NBA player-season from 1950-51 to 2025-26, in Yahoo's default points league: a point is worth 1, a rebound 1.2, an assist 1.5, a steal or block 3, and a turnover costs 1.

Fill in the stats nobody counted

The NBA didn't track steals or blocks until 1973-74, or turnovers until 1977-78. I learned the patterns from modern players (rebound-heavy big men block more shots, players with the ball a lot turn it over more) and used them to estimate the old seasons.

Check the estimates

I tested them on seasons where the real numbers are known. On average they were off by about a quarter of a steal, block or turnover a game.

Adjust for pace

Teams in the sixties had far more possessions, so more shots and rebounds to go around. Rounds 1 and 2 put every season on the same pace (per 100 possessions).

Run four rounds

Best season, best career, most fantasy titles and dominance. Each round is worth 25 points, scored relative to the round winner, for a total out of 100.

The fine print

Is it just the estimates?

The estimates have one weak spot: they probably underrate the best shot-blockers ever, like Bill Russell (about 2.3 blocks a game in my estimate, while historians guess 6 to 8).

Seasons need at least 70% of games played to count in Round 1. Data: Basketball-Reference and NBA.com; ESPN average draft position for the No. 1 pick history.
